Welcome to Juju Reference docs -- our cast of characters (tools, concepts, entities, and processes) for the Juju story!

When you install a Juju [client](/t/13146), for example the  [`juju` CLI client](/t/5465), and give Juju access to your [cloud](/t/5454) (Kubernetes or otherwise), your Juju client [bootstraps](/t/6209) a  [controller](/t/5455) into the cloud. 

From that point onward you are officially a Juju  [user](/t/6186) with a [`superuser` access level](6864#heading--controller-superuser) and therefore able to use Juju and [charms](/t/5457) or [bundles](/t/bundle-reference/1158) from our large collection on [Charmhub](/t/6680) to manage [applications](/t/5471) on that cloud.

In fact, you can also go ahead and add another cloud definition to your controller, for any cloud in our long [list of supported clouds](/t/6665).      

On any of the clouds, you can use the controller to set up a [model](/t/5456), and then use Juju for all your application management needs -- from application [deployment](/t/11285) to [configuration](/t/6659) to [constraints](/t/6184) to [scaling](/t/13137) to [high-availability](/t/1066)  to [integration](/t/5464) (within and between models and their clouds!) to [actions](/t/6208) to [secrets](/t/7286) to [upgrading](/t/1199) to [teardown](/t/1063).     

You don't have to worry about the infrastructure -- the Juju controller [agent](/t/5466) takes care of all of that automatically for you. But, if you care, Juju also lets you manually control [availability zones](/t/6695), [machines](/t/5459), [subnets](/t/6234), [spaces](/t/1157), [secret backends](/t/8702), [storage](/t/6185).
